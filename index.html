01 — Central Thesis

Conscious awareness emerges from recursively organised constraints.

ATA proposes that experience is not necessarily the creation of reality at each moment, but the result of a particular structure becoming accessible through a constrained system.

02 — Basic Formulation

The core model

ATA begins with a compact mathematical representation of constrained access:

E(t) = AC(t)(M)
Experience as an aperture-dependent mapping over an underlying structure.
M
Underlying structure
The modelled state space or structure upon which the aperture operates.
C(t)
Constraint configuration
The time-dependent organisation of constraints governing what can become accessible.
AC
Aperture mapping
A mapping determined by the relevant constraint configuration.
E(t)
Experiential state
The resulting organisation of experience at time t.

In plain language: experience is modelled as constrained access to an underlying structure.

03 — Mathematical Structure

From intuition to formalisation

The basic formulation can be expanded by specifying the underlying structure and the accessibility of different states within it.

Underlying structure

M = (X, g, Ψ)

Here, X represents an underlying state space, g represents structural or geometric information, and Ψ represents a state or field defined over that structure.

Accessibility

α(x,t;C) ∈ [0,1]

The quantity α is introduced as a candidate accessibility function. It represents the degree to which a state or region x is accessible under a particular constraint configuration.

This is a proposed formal construct rather than an established physical quantity.

Continuous formulation

E(t) = ∫M α(x,t;C(t)) Ψ(x) dμ(x)

Under a continuous representation, experiential organisation can therefore be modelled as a constraint-weighted mapping over the underlying structure.

Constraint dynamics

dC/dt = F(C,u,t)

The constraint configuration may itself evolve. The function F represents a proposed dynamical rule, with u representing relevant inputs or perturbations.

Recursive organisation

C(k+1) = Fk(C(k))

ATA uses recursive organisation to describe constraints whose structure can themselves be constrained, transformed, or embedded within higher-order organisation.

04 — Testing Programme

What could ATA predict?

A mathematical description alone does not establish a theory. The stronger question is whether the formal structure can generate predictions that distinguish ATA from alternative explanations.

Constraint measurement

Identify measurable properties of a system that could plausibly represent changes in constraint organisation.

Recursive organisation

Determine whether recursive structure can be quantified and whether such a quantity relates systematically to conscious organisation.

Temporal organisation

Investigate whether changes in constraint dynamics correspond to measurable changes in temporal characteristics of experience.

Model comparison

Compare aperture-based models against models that explain the same observations without the additional constraint structure.

Proposed testing pipeline
data → C(t) → R(C) → prediction → observed data
05 — Open Mathematical Problem

Can recursive constraint organisation be quantified?

One of the central unresolved mathematical questions in ATA is whether the proposed notion of recursive constraint organisation can be represented by a useful quantitative measure.

R : 𝒞 → ℝ

Here R(C) represents a proposed measure of recursive organisation within the space of possible constraint configurations.

Status

R(C) is an open construct. ATA does not claim that a validated measure currently exists. Finding a mathematically coherent and empirically useful definition is itself part of the research programme.

A stronger version of the hypothesis would investigate whether changes in R(C) systematically correspond to independently measurable properties of conscious organisation.

R(C) → Φ(E)

Φ(E) represents an independently measurable property of experiential or conscious organisation.

06 — Falsification

What could make ATA fail?

ATA is intended to remain open to failure. The framework would be weakened if its central concepts could not be made precise or if its proposed explanatory structure provided no advantage over existing alternatives.

  1. If constraint organisation cannot be given a coherent mathematical definition, the formal programme fails.
  2. If R(C) cannot be operationalised or measured in a reproducible way, the quantitative extension remains unsupported.
  3. If predicted relationships between constraint organisation and conscious organisation repeatedly fail, ATA loses empirical support.
  4. If existing theories explain the relevant observations equally well or better without an aperture/constraint structure, ATA's additional explanatory value is reduced.
  5. If the mathematics merely redescribes existing models without producing new consequences, the formalism may have limited theoretical significance.
A useful theory should specify not only what it could explain, but also what would count against it.
07 — Philosophical Interpretation

What follows if the framework is correct?

The philosophical implications of ATA should be distinguished from its mathematical claims. The following are interpretations and consequences explored by the framework rather than established empirical conclusions.

Temporal experience

If an underlying structure is not identical to the sequence of its access, the apparent flow of experience may reflect changing accessibility rather than the creation of each moment.

Individuality

Individual experience can be understood through the particular constraints that distinguish one biological system's access from another's.

The self

The ordinary sense of a continuous self may arise partly from the persistence and organisation of constraints across successive experiential states.

Suffering

Persistent suffering may be interpreted as a highly stable organisation of experiential constraints, rather than simply as the presence of an isolated unpleasant event.

Important distinction

These philosophical implications do not constitute evidence for the mathematical model. They are interpretations that depend upon the underlying framework being viable.

08 — Limits

What ATA does not currently establish

Not an established physical theory ATA is a developing theoretical framework and has not been experimentally validated as a theory of physics.
Not proof of consciousness beyond brains The framework does not currently demonstrate that consciousness exists independently of biological systems.
Not proof of eternalism The framework can be interpreted using an eternalist picture of spacetime, but it does not itself establish eternalism as physically true.
Not an established equation The mathematical expressions presented here are proposed formal structures for developing and testing the framework.

The distinction between mathematical definition, hypothesis, prediction and philosophical interpretation is deliberately maintained throughout the framework.
